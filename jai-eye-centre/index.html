2-minute home check

Amsler grid: check your central vision

Retina specialists use this simple grid to spot early changes from macular degeneration or diabetic eye disease.

  1. Hold your phone or screen at reading distance, in good light, wearing your reading glasses.
  2. Cover one eye and look only at the dot in the centre.
  3. Are any lines wavy, blurred, or missing? Repeat with the other eye.
All lines straight 👍

This is a screening aid, not a diagnosis. People with diabetes should have a dilated retina check every year.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Do you accept CGHS?

Yes, Jai Eye Centre is CGHS-empanelled. For planned treatment, bring a referral from your CGHS wellness centre; emergencies don't need one.

Is cashless insurance available?

We work with leading health insurers for cashless treatment. Call us with your policy details and we'll check your eligibility before your visit.

Am I a good candidate for LASIK?

That depends on your corneal thickness and shape, the stability of your prescription and your eye health. A LASIK work-up (corneal topography, pachymetry and more) tells us which option suits you: LASIK, PRK, ICL or refractive lens exchange.

When should I get my cataract operated?

When blurred or glary vision starts affecting daily life, like reading, driving or recognising faces. Modern phaco surgery is a day-care procedure; your surgeon will advise the right time and lens for you.

I have diabetes. How often should I get my eyes checked?

At least once a year, with a dilated retina examination, even if your vision feels normal. Diabetic retinopathy often has no early symptoms.

Do you check children's eyes?

Yes. We evaluate and treat squint, amblyopia (lazy eye) and refractive errors in children, and conduct school eye check-up camps.
